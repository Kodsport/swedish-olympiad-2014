\problemname{TV Watching}
Johan loves watching TV all day, but cannot stand commercial breaks. When there is a commercial break on the channel
he is watching, he switches to the next channel, and repeats this until he finds a channel without commercials. If he is watching
the last channel when this happens, he starts over from the first channel. Then he watches that channel until there is a commercial, and so on.

Johan wonders in hindsight how much he actually watched each channel during a day. Given the times when there were commercial breaks on each channel,
compute this for him. He watched between the times \texttt{00:00} and one full day ahead, and started watching the first channel.
You can assume that a channel switch takes 1 minute, and that during the switch no TV watching occurs (for each channel switch, one minute of TV watching is thus lost).

\section*{Input}
Input starts with an integer $N$ ($1 \leq N \leq 1000$), the number of channels.

Then follows one line per channel: each line starts with an integer $r_i$, the number of commercial breaks on the channel.
Then follow in chronological order $r_i$ ($0 \leq r_i \leq 1440$) commercial breaks on the same line, separated by spaces.
Each commercial break is given with a start time and end time in the format \texttt{HH:MM-HH:MM}, e.g. \texttt{05:24-22:23} means the channel had
a commercial break from \texttt{05:24} up to and including \texttt{22:23}, and it was exactly 17 hours long. Note that during minute \texttt{22:23} the commercial continues,
and it ends at \texttt{22:24}. A commercial break starts at the earliest at midnight and ends at the latest at midnight (\texttt{00:00} and \texttt{23:59}, respectively).
No commercial breaks overlap on the same channel.

\section*{Output}
Output $N$ lines. For each line $1 \leq k_i \leq N$, output a single integer: the number of minutes Johan watched channel $k_i$.

\section*{Scoring}
Your solution will be tested on a set of test groups.
To earn points for a group, you must pass all test cases in that group.

\section*{Explanation of Sample 1}
There are three channels that Johan watches. He starts immediately watching the first channel at 00:00, and immediately runs into a commercial break.
He then switches channel (which takes one minute), and unfortunately runs into a commercial break again.
He therefore switches channel again, and the same thing happens on the third channel.
After three minutes, Johan switches to the first channel again, and can finally start counting minutes of quality TV.
He now watches the first channel for the rest of the time, i.e. 1437 minutes.
